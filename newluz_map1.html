Smart Loading Zones — design methodology

Design methodology

Idea

A Smart Loading Zone is a delimited loading/unloading area equipped with technology (vehicle detection, occupancy monitoring, assignment) whose data feed decisions by authorities and operators. The methodology covers the strategic decision — how many LZ, where, and how many stalls — with machine learning plus optimisation.

Placement rule

LUZ are placed only on kerb adjacent to parking (outer / inner) and holding areas. They are never placed inside those polygons, and they are excluded from entry / exit points of those areas (the first and last 20 m of any road segment that touches the parking/holding polygon boundary is removed from the candidate set). This guarantees deliveries do not block the ingress/egress of parking and holding areas and do not consume parking stalls.

Step 1 — Greenfield analysis (number & rough location)

τ_j  = λ·μ / θ                      demand of establishment j (θ = 85 %)   Eq. 2
WSW_j = τ_j · ‖x_j − n_i‖²           weighted squared walking distance      Eq. 1
φ    = Σ_x min_n WSW                 objective minimised by the algorithm    Eq. 4
  • Weighted k-means++: centroids are seeded with probability ∝ WSW and updated as demand-weighted means, so they sit closer to freight-intense establishments.
  • DBSCAN: needs no N, only ε (max distance between neighbours); finds connected groups and noise.
  • Covering principle: increase N until the share of establishments within dmax (75 m) of a centroid meets the target. k-means++ is preferred for LZ siting because it minimises distance to the centroid; DBSCAN chains establishments into large clusters.

Step 2 — Service assignment (generalised assignment problem)

min  Σ_i Σ_j d_ij Σ_t e_ijt                              Eq. 5
s.t. Σ_i e_ijt = 1                  ∀ j, t   single sourcing      Eq. 6
     Σ_i d_ij e_ijt ≤ d_max         ∀ t      walking limit        Eq. 7
     Σ_j e_ijt τ_jt ≤ S_it          ∀ i, t   stall capacity       Eq. 8
     e_ijt ∈ {0,1},  S_it ∈ ℕ                                    Eq. 9
     S_it = ⌈ Σ_j e_ijt τ_jt ⌉      dynamic stalls per hour       Eq. 10

Candidate LZ are the kerb-feasible sites near the greenfield centroids. The model gives each establishment to one LZ and sizes every LZ hour by hour, so unused kerb can be returned to other users. If some establishments are beyond dmax, the limit is relaxed.

Step 3 — ICT

Choose the stationary technology (sensors, cameras, check-in app) that captures arrivals λ, dwell μ and occupancy, then feed the measured data back into Steps 1–2.

Sizing rule — Time-space (TRB / ITE)

Number of stalls for a zone or site:

N = ⌈ λ_peak · T_service / (60 · U_target) ⌉

λ_peak = peak-hour vehicle arrivals (veh/h); T_service = mean loading or unloading time (min); U_target = target utilisation (85 % typical). This is the curb-management standard — it conserves bay-minutes (bay-minutes demanded = bay-minutes supplied) and is directly explainable to reviewers.

Equivalently, if you already have the offered load a = λ·T_service in Erlangs:

N = ⌈ a / U_target ⌉

Walking distance — method

Real pedestrian-network distances are computed on the pedestrian graph. This explorer supports two sources:

  • Approximate — straight line × circuity factor (default 1.25). Fast, free, a proxy.
  • Real network — OpenRouteService foot-walking matrix (free tier, on the OpenStreetMap pedestrian graph).
